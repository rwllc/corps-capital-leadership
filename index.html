🚀 Rocket Fuel — The Visionary + Integrator

From Rocket Fuel by Gino Wickman & Mark C. Winters. This is the relationship that determines whether a company reaches escape velocity — or stalls. Part 1 defines the two roles. Part 2 (Chapters 4–5) is the how-to: the Accountability Chart and the 5 Rules for making the relationship work.

Part 1 — The Two Roles
The Visionary

Sees what others can't yet see

  • Generates ideas constantly — often too many
  • Sees the big picture and future direction
  • Passionate, energizing, and inspiring
  • Strong external relationships — clients, partners, press
  • Challenges the status quo; thrives on new
  • Creative problem solver when stuck
  • Not great with follow-through or routine
  • Gets bored once the idea is proven
  • Operates on instinct and big-picture thinking
  • Would rather start 10 things than finish 1
Blind Spots
  • Creates chaos without realizing it
  • Skips process; doesn't like structure
  • Hard time letting go of the vine
  • Can undermine the Integrator with "end runs"
  • Why does this need a meeting? Just do it.
The Integrator

Makes the vision real

  • Executes, operationalizes, and delivers
  • Ties the whole company together
  • Runs the leadership team and meeting cadence
  • Accountable for P&L and day-to-day operations
  • Creates clarity, consistency, and calm
  • Filters the Visionary's ideas — decides what gets done
  • Detail-oriented; respects process
  • Manages people; holds the team accountable
  • Can say "no" to protect the organization's capacity
  • Sees the whole chessboard — people, resources, timing
Blind Spots
  • Can block the Visionary's access to the company
  • May dismiss the Visionary as chaotic
  • Can over-manage and stifle initiative
  • Needs to remember: they serve the vision, not control it
The Relationship — Why Most V+I Pairs Fail

The Visionary Trap: Letting Go of the Vine

The hardest thing for a Visionary isn't the vision — it's giving up control of execution. Most Visionaries have done it themselves for so long that handing it to an Integrator feels like losing. It's not. It's the only way the company grows past the founder's personal bandwidth.

When the Visionary holds on, the Integrator can't lead. The team gets confused about who to listen to. The Visionary keeps making "end runs" — going directly to people below the Integrator — and trust breaks down in both directions.

Rocket Fuel's premise: The Visionary provides the spark. The Integrator provides the fuel. Without both, there is no thrust.

The Question Every Visionary Must Answer

What do you actually want to build — and why? Not the product, not the clients, not the revenue. What do you want this company to be in 10 years? What role do you want to play in it? What do you want to have built when you look back?

These are not abstract questions. They are the foundation of the V/TO — and they are the first thing that breaks down when a Visionary has never been asked to articulate them out loud, to their Integrator, with accountability attached.

Chapter 4 — The Accountability Chart

The Structure That Makes the Relationship Work

The Accountability Chart is not an org chart. It's a function-first map of how the company runs — and it has one non-negotiable structural rule:

  1. Visionary sits at the top. Vision, culture, major relationships, and R&D are the Visionary's domain.
  2. Integrator sits directly below. All major functions — Sales, Operations, Finance, People — report to the Integrator, not the Visionary.
  3. The Visionary does not have direct reports below the Integrator (with rare exceptions for special roles). This is the structural enforcement of the relationship.
  4. Open seats are not failures — they are clarity. A blank seat named "Ventures Lead" tells you exactly what the firm needs next.

Chapter 5 — The 5 Rules for the V+I Relationship
1

Stay on the same page

The Visionary and Integrator hold a regular Same Page Meeting — just the two of them. Issues are surfaced and resolved between them before they reach the leadership team. This is the most important habit in the relationship.

2

No end runs

The Visionary communicates to the leadership team through the Integrator — not around them. Going directly to the Integrator's direct reports with direction, tasks, or strategy undermines the Integrator's authority and creates confusion throughout the organization.

3

The Integrator is the tie-breaker

On operational decisions, the Integrator has final say. This protects the organization from the Visionary's instinct to override or second-guess execution decisions. The Visionary owns the vision; the Integrator owns the how.

4

The Integrator cannot block the Visionary from the company

The Integrator's role is not to be a gatekeeper. The Visionary must have access — to people, to clients, to culture. If the Integrator closes off the Visionary, the relationship breaks down from the other direction. Both rules 2 and 4 must be honored simultaneously.

5

Mutual respect — celebrate the differences

The Visionary thinks the Integrator is too slow. The Integrator thinks the Visionary is reckless. Both are right — and both are essential. The relationship only works when both roles are genuinely valued, not tolerated. When the V+I pair shows up as a united front, the whole team takes its cue from them.

The Same Page Meeting®

The Weekly Check-In That Keeps Everything Together

This is a standing meeting — just Visionary and Integrator, 60–90 minutes, weekly or bi-weekly. It is the single most important process discipline in the V+I relationship.

  1. What's up — What happened this week? Good news, bad news, anything important to know.
  2. Open issues — What's on the Issues List that needs resolution between the two of you, before it reaches the team?
  3. What the Integrator needs from the Visionary — Decisions, clarity, priorities.
  4. What the Visionary needs from the Integrator — Updates, access, unblocking.
  5. To-dos from last meeting — What was committed? What got done?
  6. Rocks check — On track or off track on 90-day priorities?

Cadence: Weekly is ideal. Minimum bi-weekly. If you skip it, issues accumulate and surface at the worst possible time.
